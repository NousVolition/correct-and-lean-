WHAT IS NOW PROVED
For the unchanged primary directional Hug settings, every real initial state
with both guards in [0,1], and every continuous prescribed force, has a unique
solution on any finite forward time interval. All eight states evolve together:
displacement, velocity, two material strains, two participant states and two
directional guards. The solution satisfies the differential equations at every
interior time and both guards remain in [0,1].

This goes beyond a short-time existence result: the interval can be arbitrarily
long. No finite-time breakdown occurs under these assumptions. Uniqueness means
that the same initial state, force and parameter settings give the same trajectory.
The formal statement is interval-by-interval, with agreement on overlapping
intervals; it does not assert uniqueness of arbitrary values outside an interval.

HOW THE NONLINEAR PROBLEM WAS HANDLED
The nonlinear squeeze term prevents simply assuming global Lipschitz continuity.
The proof instead establishes growing finite-time bounds for the six unrestricted
states and 0-to-1 bounds for the guards. An auxiliary bounded field constructs the
solution, and a separate proved identity removes that auxiliary projection along
the entire path. The final theorem is about the original equations.

ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITS
The theorem uses the already frozen primary parameter set, including unit weights
in both influence directions. The force must be continuous on the chosen interval,
and the initial guards must lie in [0,1]. No smallness assumption is made on the
other initial states, no trajectory bound is assumed, and no state is frozen.

It does not yet prove the same result for every other parameter/control variant,
the separate full memory-field extension, a stable rhythm, synchronization,
recovery time, physical contact, or a measured social interpretation. Numerical
solver and floating-point correctness are separate questions.
