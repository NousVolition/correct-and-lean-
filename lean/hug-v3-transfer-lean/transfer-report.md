The unchanged shared-state equation is

\[
\dot n_i=0.7(0.6I_i+0.4e_i-n_i)
 +\frac15\sum_j(r_{ij,1}+r_{ij,6}-r_{ij,4})(e_j-n_i).
\]

Here `I` is fixed intent, `e` is expression, `n` is the norm center, and response coordinates 1, 6 and 4 are cooperation, repair and coercion. These names come from the existing model. They are not measurements of speaking time or understanding.

For student `i`, first teacher `a` and second teacher `b`, changing only `r[i,a,1]` by δ gives

\[
\Delta\dot n_i=\frac{\delta}{5}(e_a-n_i).
\]

To inspect the existing guard without adding an evolution equation, define the derived quantity

\[
Q_{ib}=e^{2\ell_{ib}}-\|e_b-n_i\|^2,
\qquad Q_{ib}>0\iff e^{\ell_{ib}}-\|e_b-n_i\|>0.
\]

Lean verifies

\[
\boxed{\Delta\dot Q_{ib}=\frac{2\delta}{5}(e_b-n_i)\cdot(e_a-n_i).}
\]

For positive δ, positive alignment produces a larger instantaneous Q-rate; zero alignment produces no change in that rate. Negative alignment reverses the sign by the same identity. A larger rate can still be negative, and a positive rate can start below the boundary. The theorem named `positive_alignment_opens_guard_instantaneously` proves a **rate comparison**, despite its abbreviated name; it does not prove threshold crossing.
