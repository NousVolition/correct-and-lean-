3. Where the stronger bounds come from

WeightedClasses.MemClass, lines 107–113, quantifies: for every finite derivative order m, there exist constants C and p such that the derivative bounds hold uniformly over the family index and points, for every j <= m. MeanClass is the corresponding weighted specialization. C and p may change with m; they are not assumed uniform over every derivative order at once.

The complete explicit uses of realInverse_finiteJets are in:
• UniformFourierAlias.meanClass_realInverse: supplies loss=5 to fiberOperator_preserves_meanClass. That general proof asks hf.bounds (m+loss), at line 1002, then constructs the actual input bound for all i <= m+loss.
• PhysicalMeanDomain.meanClass_temporalInverse: asks hclass.bounds (m+5), line 1247. Fiber localization preserves the required jets, and the proof passes the resulting bound at line 1263.
• VariableGaugeMean.meanClass_temporalInverse_moving: asks hclass.bounds (m+5), line 2471, preserves those jets under localization, and passes the bound at line 2488.

Thus the conclusion follows from quantitative all-order family estimates. Smoothness by itself would not establish a uniform bound over a varying family; the code supplies the stronger quantitative property.

The two explicit uses of familyInverse_finiteJets occur in UniformFourierAlias.totalIntegral_finiteJets and exactAlias_finiteJets. Both instantiate inverse loss=5 and require m+6p source orders for p integrations by parts. Each step spends five orders on inversion and one on a radial derivative. GaugeAliasDecay.sourceJet_finiteJets_fiber makes the same arithmetic explicit: (m+1)+5+6p = m+6(p+1).

UniformFourierAlias.meanClass_alias_superflat, lines 1148–1165, chooses a finite integration order and then extracts the source estimate at m+6p. Higher-order constants/polynomial degrees are allowed by the all-order class; the same epsilon exponent is preserved by the inverse-class steps. This is genuine derivative budgeting, not an appeal to an unspecified extra derivative.

5. Independent mathematical check of the isolated m+4 estimate

Assume a smooth zero-mean function F on the unit two-torus, N = v dot grad, and the paper's nonresonance/divisor bound |v dot k|^(-1) <= C(1+|k|) for nonzero integer k. This assumption is kept explicit; the entire global construction is not inferred from it.

Set n=|k|_infinity >= 1. Select a coordinate whose frequency has magnitude n. Integrating the Fourier coefficient by parts m+4 times in that coordinate gives

  |F_hat(k)| <= (2*pi*n)^(-(m+4)) * ||F||_(C^(m+4)).

For any output multi-index alpha with |alpha| <= m, differentiation contributes at most a constant times n^m, and the inverse multiplier contributes at most a constant times n. Consequently each differentiated inverse-series term is bounded by

  C_m * ||F||_(C^(m+4)) * n^(-3).

There are exactly 8n integer lattice points with infinity norm n. The sum is therefore controlled by 8 * sum_(n>=1) n^(-2), which converges. The Weierstrass bound controls the supremum of every derivative up to order m and yields the paper's local C^m <= C_m C^(m+4) estimate. Smooth input permits the same reasoning at every order. The zero Fourier mode is fixed to zero.

The Lean coefficient_seminorm_bound instead uses a lattice majorant with fourth-power decay and requests p+4 derivatives for the p-th weighted Fourier moment. The inverse uses moment p=m+1, hence m+5. This explains the extra derivative as a conservative proof route; it is not a counterexample to the sharper m+4 bound. The hand argument above has not been formalized or compiled here.
